Vye Duel

A two-player game of capture and control
2 players · 15–20 minutes
Print and play

The goal

Build the biggest kingdom: a group of your tiles connected side by side. Every tile is double-sided. The {{MOON}} owl side belongs to House Midnight. The {{ROSE}} rose side belongs to the House of a Thousand Roses. When you capture a tile, flip it to your side.

{{SAMPLE}}

What you need

  • 21 Lands: 7 kinds, 3 of each
  • 4 Family tiles: a King and a Knave for each house
  • 3 Specials: Usurp, Lost City and Sinkhole
  • The Crossroads start tile, plus a captured Crossroads

You also need a bag or cup to draw from, and something to hide your hand behind, like a book standing up. Both sides of a tile show the same picture, so anyone can see what you’re holding.

Setup

  1. Put the Crossroads in the middle of the table. Keep the captured Crossroads nearby.
  2. Pick houses. Midnight goes first. Roses goes second.
  3. Each player puts their own King and Knave in front of them, their side up. This is your sideboard, and everyone can see it.
  4. Put the 21 Lands, Usurp, Lost City and Sinkhole in the bag: every tile without a crown in the corner. Each player draws 2 without looking.

On your turn

Play one tile, from your hand or your sideboard, and do what it does. Then draw from the bag until you hold 2 again. When the bag is empty, keep playing what you have.

Placing a tile

Put the tile in an empty space beside (not diagonal to) any tile on the table, your side up. The table can never grow past 5 tiles wide or 5 tiles tall, so there are 25 spaces in all. Cover tiles go on top of a tile instead (see Specials).

Capturing with Lands

Place a Land beside a matching Land and you capture it, along with every matching Land connected to it. Flip them all to your side. The chain runs through your own matching Lands too.

+VVG→VVVG
Midnight plays a Violet Peaks (V) beside two Roses Violet Peaks. Both flip to Midnight. The Green Hills (G) doesn’t match, so it stays.

Capturing with Family

{{KING}}
King: flips the 4 tiles beside it.
{{KNAVE}}
Knave: slips past a tile beside it and flips the tile right behind. Pick one direction.

Kin come home. When you place a Family tile beside a Family tile of the same house (blue art for Midnight, red art for Roses), you take that one too, even if your opponent captured it.

The Crossroads. Nobody owns it at the start. A Family capture that reaches it takes it: swap in the captured Crossroads, your side up. Lands never capture it, and nothing can be stacked on it.

Family tiles don’t set off Land matching, and Lands don’t set off Family captures.

Specials

Usurp. Pick a Land on the table that isn’t covered. Take it away and put a Land from your hand in its space, your side up. It matches like a normal Land play. The old Land goes back in the bag and Usurp leaves the game. You played two tiles, so draw back up to 2.

Lost City. Cover a Land you own. It’s worth 2 points in your kingdom, and it can be captured like any other tile.

Sinkhole. Cover any tile except the Crossroads. Nobody owns it and it can’t be captured. Use it to cut a kingdom in two.

Stacking. Cover tiles can go on top of any tile, even one that’s already covered. Only the Crossroads is off-limits. Only the top tile counts.

End of the game

The game ends when all 25 spaces are full, or when the player whose turn it is has nothing they can play.

Scoring

Find your biggest kingdom: your tiles connected side by side. Diagonals don’t connect. Each tile is worth 1 and a Lost City is worth 2. Only your biggest kingdom counts.

The bigger kingdom wins. Ties go to Roses, the second player, which evens out Midnight’s head start.

Tips

  • Hold your Family tiles until they can take two or more.
  • A Sinkhole between two enemy groups keeps them apart for good.
  • Count the bag. When it runs low, the tiles left in hands decide the game.

Printing

  • US Letter paper. Light cardstock works best.
  • Print at Actual size (100%), not “Fit to page.”
  • Print pages 3–8 double-sided, flip on long edge. Each even page is the back of the page before it.
  • Cut on the lines on the front of each sheet. Tiles are 2.5 inches, the width of a playing card.
