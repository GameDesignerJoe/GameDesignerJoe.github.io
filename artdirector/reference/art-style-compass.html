Step 1 of 10 — Project Identity

What are you making?

These foundational choices shape every prompt. Camera type is especially important — it determines how your character is shown and changes the art prompts significantly.

Stylized Painterly in 2D = Cuphead. In Full 3D = Dishonored. Same DNA, completely different pipelines.

Step 2 of 10 — Core Tone

How should it feel?

Tone is the most important constraint. A well-matched art style amplifies your game’s emotion — a mismatched one fights it.

Step 3 of 10 — Visual DNA (most important)

Pick one visual language

This applies to everything — characters, enemies, environments, props. Mixing these across asset types is how games feel incoherent.

Step 6 of 10 — Environments

Where does this happen?

Three environments produce three separate 16:9 shots for your pitch deck. Each should be meaningfully different from the last to show your world’s range.

Step 7 of 10 — Player Characters

Who is the player?

Player characters carry the visual identity of the whole game. Their silhouette becomes your logo.

First-person camera selected. The full player character is never shown in-game. Instead of a character portrait, you’ll get a first-person hands/arms study — what the player sees of themselves. Key art will show first-person perspective looking into the world rather than a hero pose.

Hands, arms, held items. This feeds the hands study prompt and appears in foreground of action shots.

Step 8 of 10 — Enemies

What does the player face?

One enemy type is enough to establish design direction. Same DNA as everything else — contrast comes from design choices, not a different rendering style.

One concrete enemy establishes the design direction. No need to describe the full roster.

Step 9 of 10 — Equipment & Props

What does the world contain?

A single prop and the equipment aesthetic is enough to establish design language for all world objects.

One specific concrete prop tells more than a list of generic items.

Art direction prompts

Using aspect ratios in your AI generator

Specify ratio explicitly: Midjourney uses --ar 16:9, --ar 1:1, --ar 2:3, --ar 4:1. In Gemini Imagen, set width/height in advanced options. Environment shots at 16:9 go full-bleed on pitch deck slides. Square shots work as column inserts. The 4:1 title banner fits across the top of any slide.
