# LABYRINTH — Vision & Narrative

*Working title. Design partner sessions, 2026. Author: Joe Morrissey.*

## One-Line Pitch

A first-person, node-based labyrinth you did not enter. You navigate with finite tools, map by hand, and uncover the remains of travelers who came before — not knowing that every one of them is you.

## Core Truth

**You are everyone in the maze. They are all you.**

The Child, the Cartographer, the Soldier, the Priest, the Criminal, the Caretaker — not different people lost in the same place. The same soul at different stages of the same life, or lives, cycling through. The maze is the between-place: where you go when you are not yet ready to go back.

**The Veil.** While inside, you do not recognize yourself. You find the Child's note and feel tenderness without knowing she is you. You find the Criminal's rage and feel judgment — or uncomfortable recognition. The Caretaker watches you with knowing eyes because he has seen you here before.

Every remnant is self-knowledge you are not yet ready to hold. The maze is the process of becoming ready.

## Thematic Frames (all apply)

- **Making sense of places we didn't choose.** Every past self did something different with the same predicament — mapped it, prayed in it, catalogued it, accepted it, lost themselves in it.
- **The people who came before and what they left.** Archaeology on lives. The remnants are the content, not clues to escape.
- **Memory and navigation as the same act.** Finite tools, a hand-drawn map, a symbol language pieced together. Making meaning from incomplete information — which is what memory actually is. The maze forgets nothing. You forget everything except what you wrote down.
- **The dignity of the lost.** None of them were stupid or weak. They encountered something larger than themselves. Getting lost is not a failure of character.
- **Pure atmosphere.** Solitude, weight, wonder, the uncanny beauty of a place that shouldn't exist.

## Touchstones

- **Piranesi** (Susanna Clarke) — a narrator who has adapted to the House; indifference more unsettling than malice.
- **An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge** — the entire narrative is a death-moment negotiation.
- **Jacob's Ladder** — "If you're frightened of dying and holding on, you'll see devils tearing your life away. If you've made your peace, the devils are really angels freeing you from the earth." The Caretaker is the angel wearing a demon's face because you are not ready to see him as kind.
- **Return of the Obra Dinn** — 1-bit dithered look (ours in whites/greys/blacks, colder), and the logbook that fills itself in.
- **Myst / Dungeon Master** — node-based first-person that feels deliberate, not limited.
- **The Backrooms** — the Domestic biome; liminal wrongness.

## Cast — Your Past Selves

All shuffle position and biome each run. One character = multiple remnants scattered across the maze, assembled non-linearly.

- **The Child** — Short notes, bad spelling. Brought here by someone else. Never angry. The last note is peaceful in a way that destroys you.
- **The Cartographer** — Entered believing the unknown could be mapped. Early notes meticulous, indexed, optimistic. Later notes contradict earlier ones. Her map is beautiful and wrong.
- **The Soldier** — Tactical, disciplined. Ran out of supplies. Learned respect. Last entry is a list of things he won't see again.
- **The Priest / Convert** — Believed a devout life was possible by giving himself to something other than himself. Late notes read like scripture. Enlightenment or madness: unresolved.
- **The Criminal** — Broken and bitter. Condemned himself to the maze. Rage softening into exhaustion.
- **The Archivist** — Catalogued everything. The lore backbone. Clinical until the very last entry.
- **The One Who Stayed** — No origin note. References the others. May still be alive.
- **The Caretaker** — The part of you that really knows. See below.

Characters may reference each other when co-placed in a run (the Cartographer following someone's twine; the Soldier finding the Archivist's notes useful). Procedural, not guaranteed.

## The Caretaker (formerly "the Curator")

Not a monster. A psychopomp. He **tends** the maze and tends you, whether you want it or not. He appears when you go somewhere you haven't earned. He does not kill or punish — he **redirects**. You wake up elsewhere, disoriented, with only the feeling that something looked at you with enormous patience.

His notes, if any, are the most unsettling because they are the most loving. He knows your name. He has watched you shed seven burdens across seven lives. He is waiting for the run where you make it through without him moving you.

At the center, he is there. That is the only time you see him clearly.

**Behavior loop:** Dormant → Signs (ambient shift, creatures flee) → Tracking → Manifest → Displacement (player moved to a random already-visited tile; brief static/silence; resume). Signs phase gives the player a chance to reroute. Always fair.

**Visual:** Never fully visible. A tall shape at the end of corridors that recedes. Anticipates rather than chases.

## Burdens — the Meaning of Puzzles

Thresholds are not locked by objects or symbols. They are locked by a **burden** — something carried, unresolved. The puzzle is recognizing what you're holding and putting it down.

- **Burdens are narrative inventory.** You enter carrying something abstracted: a stone, a sealed letter, a name carved in bone. You don't know what it means yet.
- **Remnants give it meaning.** The Criminal's notes tell you what he couldn't forgive. The Priest's pages show what he gave himself to instead. The weight acquires a name.
- **The threshold asks you to act.** A basin, an altar, a fire. You place the burden. It is consumed. The passage opens. The game does not explain what you just did.
- **What you leave persists.** Next run, the basin already holds something. You can examine what you left before. You begin to see the pattern of what you carry.

## Skeletons as Chrysalis

The remains you find are not corpses. They are what a past self left behind when it transcended the maze and became ready to be reborn. Their choices, solved puzzles, chalk marks, and notes persist. Each run you walk through your own history without recognizing it.

## Shape of a Run

1. **Arrival** — You appear. Behind you: warm light, a doorframe, the outside as a wash of color. You can step back to the threshold. After ~45 seconds or N moves, it becomes wall. Seamless. You may not notice for a while.
2. **Wandering** — You find past selves. You feel for them.
3. **Recognition** — A detail too close to be coincidence. The veil thins.
4. **The Threshold** — A burden confronted and set down.
5. **The Center** — The Caretaker. Silence, or one sentence. No fanfare. Just arrival.
6. **Return** — Next run. The veil is back, slightly thinner.

## Tone

Old letters found in a box. Not horror — **weight**. The maze is indifferent, which is worse than malevolent. Sparse generative music; long silences; when something plays, distant strings or solo voice. Handwritten fonts for remnant notes, serif for UI, no game-y chrome.

## Open Narrative Questions

1. How explicit is the "these are all you" revelation — stated, or assembled and never confirmed?
2. Is the entering burden fixed per run, variable, or player-chosen?
3. The Caretaker's one sentence at the center — silence, a line, or something the player writes?
4. Is there an ending that feels like completion, or does it cycle — always a little lighter?
