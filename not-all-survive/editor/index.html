    Painting the terrain

    The game guesses what every patch of your map is. Paint over anything to change it: whatever you paint replaces the automatic guess, rock and cliffs included. Anything you don’t paint keeps the guess.

    B BrushPaint the selected terrain. [ ] size.
    E EraserTwo modes, shown under the tools: Clear to snow (the default) turns anything, automatic or painted, into plain walkable snow; Restore auto removes only your own paint so the automatic guess shows again.
    G FillClick an area to repaint every connected patch of the same terrain.
    R L Rectangle, LassoDrag to fill a big area.
    Alt/⌥Hold it with the Brush, Fill, Rectangle or Lasso to erase instead of paint (using the Eraser’s mode).
    0–7Pick a terrain. I picks the terrain under the cursor.
    Move aroundScroll or pinch to zoom. Drag with Space, the right or middle mouse button, or two fingers. F shows the whole map.
    UndoCtrl/⌘+Z, redo with Shift as well.
    P Play-testOpens the game where you are looking, with your edits.

    What the terrain means in the game

      Climbing & plateaus overlay C

      Shows what the game works out from your painting: ladder stripes mark cliff faces the explorer can climb, outlined areas are plateau tops he can only reach by climbing, and grey stipple is ground he can’t reach at all. It updates a moment after you stop painting.

      Saving

      Your work saves itself in this browser as you paint, and the Play-test button uses it. To make it permanent for everyone, click Export terrain-edits.png, put the file in not-all-survive/assets/ (replacing the old one) and commit it. The editor loads that file when it opens.
