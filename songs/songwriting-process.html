A Field Guide · One Songwriter's Method

How I
Write Songs

From a blank page to a finished track — moving between human craft and AI generation, refining the words and the sound together until it lands.

Google DocsClaudeSuno
1
Free-Write First
Write freely in Google Docs about the topic — usually in paragraph form — until there's something worth shaping.
2
Anchor the Tone
Lock in a genre or artist reference to set the style and emotional register before drafting a single lyric.
3
Lock the Emotional Frame
Settle whose voice it is and what they feel. Small shifts in framing change everything, so this comes first.
4
Build Section by Section
Work with Claude iteratively — verse, chorus, pre-chorus, bridge — one piece at a time, never all at once.
5
Steer With My Own Lines
Drop in revised lines mid-session as directional signals, showing where the song should go.
6
Push Back When It Misses
Reject directions that feel wrong, flag weak or broken rhymes, and read lines aloud for rhythm and singability.
7
Feed Bits Into Suno Early
Run sections through Suno to actually hear them. Once you hear it, things click — or don't — in a way the page can't show.
8
Keep It Grounded & Tight
Favor conversational, authentic language. Cut toward one line, one idea — concrete, visceral, disciplined.
9
Hold the Lyric in a Doc
Keep the full draft in a document to avoid formatting issues and protect a clean working version.
10
Tag the Stanzas
Mark each section [VERSE], [CHORUS], [BRIDGE] so Suno knows how to build it. The basic structural tags are what reliably work.
11
Dial In the Style Tags
Write specific, production-focused tags — no artist names, phonetic spelling for tricky words. Claude helps, but hands-on exploration dials it in.
12
Generate, Listen, Rewrite
25 to nearly 100 generations before it's ready. Rewrite the lyrics dramatically to hit harder against what Suno produces — words and sound evolve together.
